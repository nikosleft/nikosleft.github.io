Apartment Panic - 3D Gameplay Prototype

A Godot 4 Forward+ first-person prototype about cleaning up a messy apartment and hiding the evidence before an inspector arrives. A single-player exploration of walkable space, physics interaction, and timed cleanup - not a shipped game.

01 / Environment Design

A complete four-room apartment, plus the outside.

The prototype is built around a fully walkable apartment: living room, kitchen, bedroom, and bathroom arranged around a compact central transition, with an exterior presentation visible through the windows. Direct routes and distinct room palettes keep navigation legible during repeated cleanup runs.

Room composition keeps each space readable at walking height: the living room is the main staging area, the kitchen is bright and open, and the bathroom and bedroom are tighter, more private spaces. Door placement, furniture lanes, and partial views between rooms were iterated around first-person movement and carrying objects through the apartment.

02 / Gameplay Systems

Clean up before the inspector arrives.

First-person movement and interaction prompts drive every run. Physics objects can be grabbed, carried, dropped, and thrown. Trash is carried to a disposal bin, a spill can be cleaned, and suspicious evidence can be placed inside a hiding cabinet. Doors choose their opening direction from the side where the player interacts, helping movement through the apartment feel physical rather than purely scripted.

The objective structure tracks trash disposal, a cleanable mess, and whether evidence is exposed. A countdown pressures the cleanup, after which an inspector follows an authored route through the apartment and produces outcome feedback from the remaining problems. The inspector is a scripted prototype behavior rather than a complete simulation, and the current build is single-player.

03 / 3D Art & Rendering

Custom props over a reusable asset base.

The apartment combines an authored room shell and custom low-poly gameplay assets with integrated reusable furniture and prop sets. Custom batches cover cleanup objects, tools, characters, clutter, floor surfaces, and interaction assets; secondary furniture, kitchenware, books, and plants complete the dressing. Final scale, placement, collision, and composition were authored for the first-person space.

The asset path runs from Blender to GLB and into Godot, with consistent scale and orientation across reusable asset batches. Rendering uses Forward+ with authored materials and lights, a procedural sky and exterior atmosphere, SSAO for contact and depth, and restrained glow around emissive accents. Lighting was balanced room by room while keeping the apartment visually coherent.

04 / Iteration & Technical Work

Smoke tests and visual passes.

Lightweight capture automation and a gameplay smoke path supported iteration. The smoke path verifies a trash disposal, spill cleanup, evidence hiding, and an inspector outcome; repeatable camera views made room composition and visual changes easier to compare.

Visual iteration covered room layout, prop placement, material albedos, direct and ambient lighting, procedural sky settings, SSAO, glow, and tonemapping. The project was also moved into Godot’s Forward+ renderer and calibrated for its HDR/linear rendering path.

05 / Status

Prototype / exploration - not shipped.

Apartment Panic is a single-player prototype and is not shipped. It was built to explore cleanup-and-hide mechanics, walkable apartment composition, and first-person physics in Godot 4.

  • Working today: full four-room apartment plus exterior, first-person movement, grab/carry/throw, side-aware doors, countdown, and a scripted inspector route with outcome feedback.
  • Not developed further: deeper evidence scoring, more inspector variation, audio, multiplayer, and a broader game loop.
